The Human Skills: Mastering the Non-AI Capabilities That Define Success in the Age of Artificial Intelligence
History Repeats: Technology Transforms, But People Drive It
Every major transformation—from the agricultural shift to the industrial revolution, to the internet and mobile age—has required people to adapt, but not everyone succeeds. In fact, 70% of large-scale transformations fail to produce expected results, and the leading cause isn't the new technology—it’s people-related challenges: misaligned communication, poor change management, and lack of shared understanding.
AI is no different. In fact, it raises the stakes.
While some worry that human cognitive offloading to AI may diminish human abilities like creativity or critical thinking, research shows the opposite—when foundational skills are nurtured, technology tends to enhance human potential, not replace it. AI won't replace thought—it will demand better thinking, and it’s essential as a society that we reinforce the importance of foundational human skills. 
In the last edition of AI Signals, we examined how AI is transforming talent across entry-level, mid-career, and executive roles. 
Amazon’s founder Jeff Bezos’s key business philosophy revolves around identifying and focusing on the things that don’t change over time, rather than getting caught up in fleeting trends. He believed that building business around these stable, unchanging factors allows for long-term strategic planning and sustainable success. 
Now, let’s zoom in on a question that often gets overlooked in the AI skill rush:
What are the foundational skills that will remain essential to enhance human potential —perhaps even more so—in an AI-first world?
